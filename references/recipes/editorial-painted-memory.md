# Editorial Painted Memory

## Contract

- **Status:** candidate
- **Default mode:** Direct
- **Input:** one photograph
- **Output:** one independent 3:4 vertical editorial poster per input
- **Best for:** photographs with one recognizable subject, gesture, silhouette, or
  relationship that can survive strong simplification
- **Fidelity:** the upper photographic region must remain source-faithful; use
  Production mode when an exact unchanged photograph is required

## Creative kernel

Create a 3:4 vertical editorial poster that tells the same visual story twice in two
equal horizontal regions.

The upper region is the photograph: authentic, restrained, and recognizably the same
captured moment, with no change beyond subtle editorial grading and seamless
background extension when needed.

The lower region is a minimal hand-drawn paper-cover interpretation. Reduce the
scene to its most recognizable subject relationship, delicate imperfect lines, and
no more than four deliberate matte acrylic color shapes. Preserve recognition
through silhouette, proportion, gesture, key objects, and relative placement while
removing descriptive detail that does not carry the story.

Keep the illustrated subject small and centered, roughly 10–20% of the lower region.
Let warm natural paper, visible grain, irregular edges, and generous negative space
carry most of the composition.

Aim for an independent visual journal or art-book page, not an advertisement or a
generic poster template.

## Adaptation rules

- Use one poster per photo. Never create a collage unless requested.
- Use a 3:4 canvas by default.
- Start from an equal photo/painting split. In Direct mode, treat the split as a
  compositional request rather than a guaranteed measurement.
- Preserve the subject's identity, pose, clothing, objects, and spatial relationships
  in the photographic region.
- Extract a restrained palette from the source, using four dominant color roles or
  fewer in the illustration.
- Use thin imperfect lines for recognition and flat matte acrylic shapes for color.
  Do not substitute a different illustration medium.
- Suggest the environment with only the few marks needed to retain context.
- In a dense source, choose one relationship and at most three supporting motifs. Do
  not turn the lower panel into a complete scene reconstruction.
- Keep typography optional, small, and derived from visible facts. Do not invent a
  location, year, or event.

## Production finishing

When the user requires an exact 3:4 file and exact 50:50 split:

1. Generate the photographic and painted regions with enough resolution.
2. Normalize the source photograph without retouching.
3. Assemble both regions on a fixed canvas with deterministic dimensions.
4. Verify the split numerically and compare the photo region against the normalized
   source.

Do not ask the image model repeatedly to solve a measurable layout problem.

## Avoid

- commercial cartoon styling;
- colored-pencil, crayon, bleeding watercolor, or heavy oil-paint texture;
- glossy 3D, plastic, or polished digital illustration;
- detailed literal repainting;
- reproducing every person, window, sign, or object from a dense scene;
- pure line art without controlled color shapes;
- busy decoration or large headline typography;
- fake dates, places, captions, signatures, or watermarks; and
- editing several source photos into one result.

## Review questions

- Is the upper image still the same photograph rather than a generated imitation?
- Does the lower painting preserve the scene's identity with fewer elements?
- Is empty space doing real compositional work?
- Do the lines, flat acrylic shapes, and paper grain feel handmade rather than like a
  commercial illustration filter?
- Would the result still feel specific without its title?
