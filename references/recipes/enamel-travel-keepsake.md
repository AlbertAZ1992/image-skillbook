# Enamel Travel Keepsake

## Contract

- **Status:** draft
- **Default mode:** Production
- **Input:** one photograph with a memorable person, landmark, or landscape motif
- **Output:** one strict 3:4 poster with an enamel keepsake above and the authentic
  source photograph below
- **Fidelity:** the lower photograph remains source-faithful; the magnet simplifies
  one decisive subject without replacing it

## Creative kernel

Pair a real memory with its small collectible emblem. Keep the lower half as the
authentic photograph. In the upper half, isolate the single person, landmark, or
landscape motif that carries the memory and reduce it to a premium enamel magnet on a
clean source-derived color field.

The large field of negative space makes the magnet feel precious and editorial. The
object should remain small, designed, and manufacturable rather than becoming a full
scene, generic badge, or standalone catalog render.

## Adaptation rules

- Use one 3:4 vertical poster with two equal horizontal regions.
- Preserve the lower photographic region; crop naturally and never stretch it.
- Fill the upper region with one clean solid color derived from the photograph.
- Select one memorable subject class: Person, Landmark, or Landscape.
- Keep the centered magnet to roughly 20–30% of the full poster width.
- Derive the outer contour from the selected subject's strongest silhouette.
- Use three to six enamel color regions sampled from the photograph.
- Preserve one or two identity cues; omit windows, foliage, and cloud micro-detail.
- Keep metal edging narrow and consistent.
- Use soft directional light and a small contact shadow to communicate thickness.
- Place an optional short handwritten English label below the magnet only when the
  user supplies it or the subject name is unambiguous.

## Production finishing

Generate only the upper keepsake panel when exact source preservation matters. Assemble
it above an unchanged normalized source crop and verify the final 3:4 canvas and 50:50
horizontal split.

## Avoid

- arbitrary city names or dates;
- thick toy-like plastic borders;
- generic shield, circle, or rectangle shapes unrelated to the source;
- excessive bevels, chrome, glitter, or gemstone effects;
- a full photographic texture mapped onto the object; and
- packaging, hands, props, multiple products, or a neutral tabletop product scene.

## Review questions

- Is the source scene recognizable from the silhouette and color structure?
- Could the object plausibly be manufactured as an enamel magnet or pin?
- Is it clearly a 3:4 photo-and-keepsake poster with equal halves?
- Is the magnet small enough to leave generous upper-panel negative space?
- Did simplification improve the design rather than erase its identity?
