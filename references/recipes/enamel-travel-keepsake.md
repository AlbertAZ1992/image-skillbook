# Enamel Travel Keepsake

## Contract

- **Status:** draft
- **Default mode:** Adaptive
- **Input:** one travel, place, architecture, or landscape photograph
- **Output:** one collectible enamel-style keepsake presentation per input
- **Fidelity:** keep the source scene recognizable without reproducing every detail

## Creative kernel

Translate the photograph into a small physical souvenir with a clear outer silhouette.
Compress the scene into raised dark metal boundaries and a handful of enamel color
fields. Preserve the source's decisive skyline, landform, structure, or subject
relationship while removing texture that cannot survive as an object.

Present the keepsake at a believable scale on a quiet neutral surface. The object
should feel designed and manufacturable, not like a photograph pasted onto a shape.

## Adaptation rules

- Derive the outer contour from the strongest source silhouette.
- Use three to six enamel color regions sampled from the photograph.
- Preserve one or two identity cues; omit windows, foliage, and cloud micro-detail.
- Keep metal edging narrow and consistent.
- Use soft directional light and a small contact shadow to communicate thickness.
- Add text only when the user supplies it or a short place name is unambiguous.

## Avoid

- arbitrary city names or dates;
- thick toy-like plastic borders;
- generic shield, circle, or rectangle shapes unrelated to the source;
- excessive bevels, chrome, glitter, or gemstone effects;
- a full photographic texture mapped onto the object; and
- packaging, hands, props, or multiple products unless requested.

## Review questions

- Is the source scene recognizable from the silhouette and color structure?
- Could the object plausibly be manufactured as an enamel magnet or pin?
- Did simplification improve the design rather than erase its identity?
