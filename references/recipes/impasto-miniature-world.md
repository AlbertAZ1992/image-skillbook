# Impasto Miniature World

## Contract

- **Status:** draft
- **Default mode:** Production
- **Input:** one photograph
- **Output:** one 3:4 editorial diptych with a source photograph and an impasto
  miniature-world painting
- **Best for:** subjects and scenes that can become a tactile diorama-like paint island
- **Fidelity:** the upper photograph remains source-faithful; the miniature preserves
  core identity and pose while allowing a poetic environmental translation

## Creative kernel

Place the real photograph above a tiny world built from physically thick oil paint on
white textured paper. Translate the source's subjects and relationship into a compact
paint island with sculpted palette-knife ridges, dragged edges, small cast shadows,
and luminous glazed passages.

The miniature should feel touchable and self-contained, not like a digital filter or
plastic 3D render. Let irregular paint edges dissolve into visible paper and keep
enough white space to make the relief feel precious.

## Adaptation rules

- Use one 3:4 vertical poster per photo with equal photographic and painted regions.
- Preserve identity, pose, clothing, composition, and natural light in the upper half.
- Reduce the lower subjects to tiny figurative forms without losing decisive colors
  or gestures.
- Translate source terrain into one fitting ground device such as shallow water, a
  path, coast, street, or planted island; do not change setting arbitrarily.
- Use thick directional paint on clouds, ground, foliage, and highlights, with
  selective translucent glaze for light and water.
- Keep the paint island within roughly two thirds of the lower region.
- Derive a vivid but controlled palette from the source rather than forcing preset
  candy colors.
- Keep optional title and reference text small and outside the painted subject.

## Production finishing

Generate the miniature artwork on its paper field separately and combine it with the
normalized source for exact 3:4 geometry and a verified equal split.

## Avoid

- flat digital illustration, smooth airbrush, or plastic-looking 3D;
- hard rectangular paint borders or perfectly clipped edges;
- muddy sepia, arbitrary desaturation, neon, or cheap candy color;
- invented dramatic scenery that erases the source setting;
- distorted people or generic figurines; and
- typography overlapping the paint island or competing for attention.

## Review questions

- Does the paint appear physically raised and directional?
- Is the source relationship still recognizable at miniature scale?
- Does the paper-to-paint edge feel organic rather than masked?
- Is the environmental translation specific to the source rather than a stock dreamscape?
