# Painted Editorial Reconstruction

## Contract

- **Status:** candidate
- **Default mode:** Production
- **Input:** one photograph
- **Output:** one 3:4 photo-and-paint editorial poster per input
- **Best for:** scenes whose gesture, relationship, or directional flow can become a
  broad painterly composition
- **Fidelity:** the photographic half remains faithful; the painted half may
  reorganize secondary forms while preserving the scene's central relationship

## Creative kernel

Keep the photograph as documentation, then rebuild its emotional structure with
paint. Distill the lower scene into a clear focal relationship, a few broad color
planes, visible matte brushwork, layered depth, and deliberate negative space.

The painting should be recognizable without behaving like a traced copy. Use the
source's gesture and spatial rhythm as scaffolding, then allow gouache, acrylic, soft
pastel, or restrained palette-knife texture to create a calm editorial image.

## Adaptation rules

- Use one 3:4 vertical poster per photograph with equal upper and lower regions.
- Preserve identity, pose, structure, light, and atmosphere in the photographic half.
- Identify one relationship or movement that deserves to anchor the reconstruction.
- Remove repetitive background detail and group secondary forms into larger shapes.
- Allow the painted focal subject to sit off-center, crop, or change scale when that
  strengthens the reading without changing its identity.
- Derive an airy color system from the source; use a small accent only when it helps
  the focal relationship.
- Keep optional editorial text quiet, specific, and subordinate to the artwork.

## Production finishing

Generate the lower painting independently, then assemble it with a normalized source
photograph when the exact 3:4 canvas and 50:50 division are required.

## Avoid

- literal tracing or full-scene detail reproduction;
- sticker collage, hard vector geometry, airbrush, plastic 3D, or photoreal painting;
- muddy filters, harsh contrast, or indiscriminate pastel wash;
- template titles, fixed numbering, invented locations, or large headlines; and
- decoration that competes with the central visual relationship.

## Review questions

- Does the lower image preserve the photograph's story while clearly becoming paint?
- Is the composition carried by hierarchy and shape rhythm rather than detail?
- Are brushwork, pigment, and edges materially convincing?
- Does the result feel like an art publication rather than a generic illustration?
