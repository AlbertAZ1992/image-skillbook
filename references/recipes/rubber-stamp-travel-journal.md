# Rubber Stamp Travel Journal

## Contract

- **Status:** draft
- **Default mode:** Production
- **Input:** one place, architecture, city, coast, or landscape photograph
- **Output:** one photograph-and-stamped-paper editorial spread per input
- **Best for:** scenes with a distinctive skyline, structure, terrain contour, road,
  coastline, or foreground silhouette
- **Fidelity:** the photographic region remains source-faithful; the stamp compresses
  rather than reproduces the scene

## Creative kernel

Pair a real place with the mark it might leave in a field journal. Keep one half as
photography and reduce the other half to a small multi-color rubber-stamp impression
on aged warm paper.

Build recognition from the few location-specific contours that matter. Print them as
two to four imperfect ink layers with carved marks, dry gaps, uneven pressure, and
slight registration drift. Surround the stamp with substantial unprinted paper so it
feels collected and observed rather than branded.

## Adaptation rules

- Choose portrait 3:4 or landscape 4:3 from the requested delivery and source.
- Use equal adjacent regions: top/bottom for portrait or left/right for landscape.
- Preserve source subjects, lighting, spatial relationships, and realistic texture in
  the photo region; crop naturally and never stretch.
- Reduce architecture to its decisive roof, arch, tower, facade, or silhouette.
- Reduce landscapes to dominant terrain, shoreline, vegetation, road, and horizon
  relationships.
- Place the stamp in the middle-to-lower paper area at a modest scale.
- Use two to four restrained source-derived inks.
- Add a small field-note label only from facts the user supplies or the image clearly
  establishes. Do not guess a location or year.

## Production finishing

Generate the stamp on its paper field separately when exact source preservation or
equal regions matter. Assemble the final spread deterministically and verify canvas,
split, orientation, and unchanged source region.

## Avoid

- circular seals, postal perforations, wax seals, stickers, or souvenir templates;
- smooth vector logos or fully illustrated landscapes;
- generic city icons and repeated micro-detail;
- perfectly registered, uniformly inked digital edges;
- decorative copy, invented geography, or misspelled labels; and
- redrawing or creatively rebuilding the photographic region.

## Review questions

- Can the place be recognized from a few scene-specific contours?
- Does the print look pressed into paper rather than filtered on screen?
- Is empty paper an active part of the composition?
- Does the chosen orientation preserve the source's strongest relationship?
